## What it is
Cancer-related fatigue is a deep tiredness that rest and sleep don't fully fix. It is one of the most common effects of cancer and its treatment, including chemotherapy, radiotherapy and immunotherapy, and it can continue for weeks or months after treatment ends. It is not laziness or weakness, and it is not a sign that the person isn't trying.

## What can help
Pacing matters more than pushing through: plan the most important activity for the time of day when energy is usually best, and break tasks into smaller pieces with rests in between. Gentle, regular movement such as a short walk often helps fatigue more than complete rest, as long as the treatment team agrees it is safe. Drinking enough fluid, eating small regular meals and keeping a steady sleep routine can also make a difference.

## When to tell the treatment team
Tell the team if fatigue suddenly gets much worse, stops the person from doing basic daily activities, or comes with breathlessness, dizziness, a racing heart or fever. Some causes of fatigue, such as anaemia, low thyroid levels, pain, poor sleep, low mood or medication side effects, can be treated, so it is always worth raising.
