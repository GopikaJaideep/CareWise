## Constipation
Pain medicines (especially opioids), anti-nausea medicines, less activity and eating or drinking less can all cause constipation. Drinking enough fluid, gentle movement and fibre (if the team agrees) can help, and the team may recommend a laxative, particularly alongside opioid pain relief. Tell the team if there has been no bowel movement for three days or more, or if there is tummy pain, swelling or vomiting.

## Diarrhoea
Some chemotherapy, immunotherapy and radiotherapy to the pelvis or abdomen can cause diarrhoea. Drinking plenty of fluid to replace what is lost is important. Small bland meals and avoiding greasy, spicy or very high-fibre foods may help. Don't use anti-diarrhoea medicine without checking with the team, because with some treatments diarrhoea needs specific management.

## When to call promptly
Call the team promptly for diarrhoea more than four to six times a day, diarrhoea with fever, blood in the stool, severe cramping, or signs of dehydration such as dizziness or very little urine. With immunotherapy, any new or worsening diarrhoea should be reported straight away.
