title: Appetite loss and eating during treatment
source: Cancer Council Australia

## Why appetite drops
Treatment, taste changes, a sore mouth, nausea, constipation, tiredness and low mood can all reduce appetite. Food that used to be enjoyable may taste metallic, bland or too strong. It is common, and it is not something the person is doing wrong.

## Making eating easier
Small, frequent meals and snacks are usually more manageable than three large meals. Choosing foods and drinks that pack in energy and protein, such as milky drinks, smoothies, eggs, yoghurt or nut butters, helps make each mouthful count. Keeping easy snacks within reach and eating when appetite is best, often earlier in the day, can help. Pressure at mealtimes tends to make things harder, so offering rather than insisting is often kinder for both of you.

## Getting help
Ask the treatment team for a referral to a dietitian, who can suggest nutritional supplement drinks and tailor advice to the person's treatment. Tell the team about ongoing weight loss, difficulty swallowing, or if the person is barely eating or drinking.
