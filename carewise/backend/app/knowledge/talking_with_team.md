title: Getting the most from appointments
source: Cancer Council Australia

## Before the appointment
Write down questions beforehand and put the most important ones first. Keep one notebook or note on your phone with symptoms, side effects, medicines and questions between appointments, so you have the details in one place. Bringing a list of current medicines, including vitamins and supplements, is useful.

## Questions worth asking
Useful questions include: What is the aim of this treatment? What side effects should we watch for, and which ones need an urgent call? Who do we call after hours? How will we know if the treatment is working? What support services are available for us?

## During and after
Bringing a second person helps, because it is hard to remember everything when you are worried. It is fine to ask the doctor to slow down, explain again, or write things down, and to ask whether you can record the conversation. Afterwards, write a short summary while it is fresh. Specialist cancer nurses are often a good first point of contact for questions between appointments.
