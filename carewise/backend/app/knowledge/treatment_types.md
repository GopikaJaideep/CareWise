## Chemotherapy
Chemotherapy uses drugs to kill cancer cells or slow their growth. It travels through the bloodstream, so it can reach cancer cells in many parts of the body. It is usually given in cycles, with rest periods in between so the body can recover, and it can be given through a drip, as tablets or by injection.

## Radiotherapy
Radiotherapy uses targeted radiation, usually from a machine outside the body, to destroy cancer cells in a specific area. Because it is aimed at one area, its side effects mostly affect the part of the body being treated. Treatment is often given daily on weekdays over several weeks.

## Other treatments
Immunotherapy helps the body's own immune system recognise and attack cancer. Targeted therapy acts on specific features of cancer cells. Hormone therapy slows cancers that rely on hormones to grow. Surgery removes cancer from the body. Many people have a combination, and the treatment team chooses based on the type and stage of cancer and the person's overall health.
