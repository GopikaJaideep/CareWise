## What to expect
Some chemotherapy drugs cause hair loss and some don't. When it happens, it usually starts two to three weeks after the first treatment and can affect the head, eyebrows, eyelashes and body hair. Radiotherapy only causes hair loss in the area being treated. Hair usually starts to grow back a few months after treatment ends, sometimes with a different colour or texture.

## Coping with it
Hair loss can be one of the most emotionally difficult visible effects of treatment. Some people cut their hair short beforehand so the change is less sudden. Soft hats, scarves and wigs are options, and some hospitals and cancer organisations can help with the cost of a wig. A gentle, mild shampoo and sun protection for the scalp help protect sensitive skin.

## Asking the team
Ask the treatment team whether the planned treatment is likely to cause hair loss, and whether scalp cooling is available and suitable, as it can reduce hair loss with some treatments.
