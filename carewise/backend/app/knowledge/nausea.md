## Why it happens
Some chemotherapy drugs, radiotherapy to the abdomen or brain, other medicines, constipation and anxiety can all cause nausea and vomiting. How much someone is affected varies a lot from person to person, and it is often easier to prevent than to stop once it has started.

## Anti-nausea medicine
Treatment teams usually prescribe anti-nausea (anti-emetic) medicine to be taken on a schedule, not only once nausea begins. If the prescribed medicine isn't working, or causes side effects, tell the team: there are several types, and switching or combining them often helps. Don't change doses or stop medicine without checking with the team first.

## Practical tips
Small meals or snacks every few hours are usually easier than large meals. Bland, dry or cold foods tend to smell less. Sipping fluids slowly through the day, ginger, fresh air, and avoiding strong cooking smells can help. Rinsing the mouth before and after eating may reduce unpleasant tastes.

## When to call the team
Call the treatment team promptly if the person can't keep fluids down for a day, is vomiting repeatedly, shows signs of dehydration such as very little urine, dizziness or confusion, or has vomiting with a fever or severe tummy pain.
