title: Fever and infection risk during chemotherapy
source: Cancer Council Australia

## Why fever matters
Chemotherapy can lower the number of white blood cells (neutrophils) that fight infection. This is called neutropenia. With neutropenia an infection can become serious very quickly, sometimes before other symptoms appear.

## What to do about a fever
A temperature of 38°C or higher during chemotherapy, or feeling suddenly very unwell even without a temperature, can be a medical emergency. Call the treatment team's 24-hour or emergency number straight away, or go to the nearest emergency department, and tell them the person is having chemotherapy. Don't wait to see if it settles, and don't give paracetamol to bring the temperature down before speaking to the team, because it can hide a fever. In an emergency in Australia, call 000.

## Other warning signs
Shivering or shaking, a cough, shortness of breath, burning when passing urine, new pain, confusion, or redness and swelling around a line or port can also be signs of infection and should be reported promptly.

## Reducing the risk
Regular hand washing for everyone in the home, avoiding people who are unwell, careful food hygiene, and asking the team about vaccinations for the person and their household can all help lower infection risk.
